> *"Before GPT learned positions, the original Transformer represented them using mathematics."*

---

## Quick Recap

Previously we learned that GPT uses **learned positional embeddings**.

<FlowDiagram title="Learned Positional Embedding" steps={[
  "Position ID",
  "Position Embedding Matrix",
  "Position Vector (learned)"
]} />

These vectors are learned during training. But the original Transformer paper (2017) asked a different question:

> **Can we represent positions without learning another embedding matrix?**

---

## The Motivation

With learned embeddings, every position needs its own vector. Researchers wondered — can mathematics generate these vectors automatically, with no extra parameters?

---

## The Idea

Instead of storing one vector per position, compute it using mathematical functions.

Every position becomes a unique pattern generated by **Sine** and **Cosine** — no additional parameters need to be learned.

## Why Sine and Cosine?

Sine and cosine are smooth periodic functions that naturally produce different values at different positions.

## One Wave Isn't Enough

Using only one sine wave, some distant positions may produce similar values — the model could confuse them.

The Transformer solves this by combining many waves at **different frequencies**.

  title="Why Multiple Frequencies?"

  right={{ label: "Multiple Frequencies", points: ["Each frequency captures a different scale", "Every position gets a unique signature", "No two positions look identical"] }}
/>

## Formula (High Level)

The original paper computes positional encoding using:

<MathBlock title="Sinusoidal Positional Encoding">
  $$PE_{(pos,\,2i)} = \sin\!\left(\frac{pos}{10000^{2i/d}}\right) \quad PE_{(pos,\,2i+1)} = \cos\!\left(\frac{pos}{10000^{2i/d}}\right)$$
</MathBlock>

where **pos** is the token position, **i** is the dimension index, and **d** is the embedding dimension.

> Even dimensions use sine, odd dimensions use cosine. The denominator `10000^(2i/d)` creates the different frequencies.

## Engineering Insight

Modern GPT models use **learned positional embeddings** (or newer variants like Rotary Position Embeddings / RoPE).

The original Transformer used sinusoidal encoding because it required no extra parameters and could generalize to sequence lengths longer than those seen during training.

---

## Key Takeaways

- The original Transformer generated position vectors mathematically — no learning required.
- Sine and cosine functions naturally produce unique patterns for each position.
- Multiple frequencies ensure no two positions look identical.
- Nearby positions have similar encodings; distant positions diverge.
- Modern GPT models mostly use learned positional embeddings instead.
