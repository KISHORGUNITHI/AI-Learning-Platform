## The Problem

Imagine the sentence:

> **The animal didn't cross the street because it was tired.**

What does **"it"** refer to?

Most humans instantly answer **The animal** without thinking — because we automatically look at surrounding words.

But does GPT know this? Not yet.

## Why Can't Embeddings Solve This?

An embedding stores the **general meaning** of a word learned during training.

For the word `bank`, the embedding captures:

- money, finance, institution
- river bank, side of a river

Now consider two sentences:

- `The bank approved my loan.`
- `The fisherman sat near the bank.`

<TokenMapping
  title="Same Embedding — Different Meaning"
  fromLabel="Sentence"
  toLabel="Embedding for 'bank'"
  items={[
    { from: "bank approved my loan",    to: "[0.42, -0.81, 0.93, ...]" },
    { from: "sat near the river bank",  to: "[0.42, -0.81, 0.93, ...]" },
  ]}
/>

Exactly the same vector enters the Transformer — but the meaning is completely different.

---

## Fixed Representations

An embedding retrieved from the embedding matrix is called a **Fixed Representation**.

Every time GPT sees `Dog`, it retrieves the exact same vector — regardless of context.

<FlowDiagram title="Fixed Representation" steps={[
  "Embedding Matrix → Dog → [0.84, -0.15, 0.27, ...]",
  "Dog barked louder  →  same vector",
  "Dog bit the man    →  same vector",
  "Dog is a guide     →  same vector",
  "Dog slept quietly  →  same vector",
]} />

Same vector. Every context. No difference.

---

## Why Is This a Problem?

Language is contextual. The meaning of a word depends on the words around it.

<Comparison
  title="The Same Word — Two Meanings"
  left={{
    label: "Financial Context",
    points: [
      "He deposited money in the bank.",
      "Surrounding: money, deposit",
      "Correct meaning: financial institution",
    ]
  }}
  right={{
    label: "Geographic Context",
    points: [
      "They sat beside the river bank.",
      "Surrounding: river, sat",
      "Correct meaning: side of a river",
    ]
  }}
/>

GPT sees the same embedding in both cases. It has no mechanism yet to decide which meaning fits.

---

## The Limitation of Fixed Representations

<Comparison
  title="What Embeddings Answer vs What They Can't"
  left={{
    label: "Embeddings Answer ✓",
    points: [
      "Who am I? (general identity)",
      "What meanings could I have?",
      "What are my typical contexts?",
    ]
  }}
  right={{
    label: "Embeddings Cannot Answer ✗",
    points: [
      "Who am I after looking at everyone else?",
      "Which meaning fits THIS sentence?",
      "What role do I play right now?",
    ]
  }}
/>

Embeddings only solve the first part. Something else must solve the second.

---

## The Big Insight

Researchers realized that instead of giving every word a fixed meaning, the model should allow each word to:

<FlowDiagram title="What Self-Attention Enables" steps={[
  "Look at other words in the sentence",
  "Gather useful information from them",
  "Understand the current context",
  "Update its own representation",
  "Know what it means in THIS sentence"
]} />

This idea became **Self-Attention**.

---

## Engineering Insight

Think of embeddings as a person's résumé.

A résumé tells us education, skills, and experience. But when that person joins a team, their role depends on everyone else in the room.

Similarly — an embedding provides general knowledge, while Self-Attention determines the token's role in the current sentence.

## Key Takeaways

- Embeddings store general semantic knowledge.
- Every occurrence of a word initially receives the same embedding.
- This is called a fixed representation.
- Natural language depends heavily on context.
- Embeddings alone cannot resolve contextual meaning.
- Researchers invented Self-Attention to solve this problem.
- Self-Attention creates context-aware representations.

## What's Next?

Now that we understand **why Self-Attention was needed**, the next question naturally arises.

If every token needs to gather information from other tokens — **how does a token know which other tokens are important?**

To answer that, researchers gave every token three special roles: **Query**, **Key**, and **Value**.

These three ideas power the entire Self-Attention mechanism, and we'll build them from first principles in the next lesson.
