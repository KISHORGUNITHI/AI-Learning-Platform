> *"Self-Attention lets tokens communicate. But should every relationship in a sentence be learned by just one attention mechanism?"*

---

## Quick Recap

We now have a complete Self-Attention mechanism. For every token, GPT computes Q, K, V and produces one contextual representation.

The obvious question:

> **If Self-Attention already works, why did the Transformer invent Multi-Head Attention?**

---

## The Problem

Consider these three sentences:

<TokenMapping
  title="Different Sentences, Different Relationship Types"
  fromLabel="Sentence"
  toLabel="Key Relationship"
  items={[
    { from: "The animal didn't cross because it was tired", to: "it → animal  (pronoun resolution)"     },

These are **completely different kinds of relationships**.

Yet a single attention head has only one set of learnable parameters. Can one mechanism become an expert in every possible relationship?

---

## Intuition

Imagine interviewing a candidate with only one interviewer who must judge programming, mathematics, communication, system design, ML, cloud, and leadership.

One person may perform reasonably well — but expecting them to be the best expert in every field is unrealistic.

## Researchers Observed Something Fascinating

When researchers visualized trained Transformer models, different attention heads naturally specialized — **nobody explicitly programmed these behaviors, they emerged automatically during training**.

The model discovered these useful behaviors simply by minimizing prediction error during training.

---

## Key Takeaways

- A single attention head produces one attention pattern.
- Natural language contains many different relationships simultaneously.
- One attention mechanism cannot specialize equally well in every relationship.
- During training, different attention heads naturally become specialists.
- The next lesson explores how multiple heads work together efficiently without dramatically increasing computation.
