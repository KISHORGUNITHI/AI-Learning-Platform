## The Problem

Consider these two sentences:

- `Dog bites man.`
- `Man bites dog.`

Both sentences contain **exactly the same words**. Yet they describe two completely different situations.

Why? Because the **order of the words changed**.

## Why Can't Embeddings Solve This?

Embeddings tell us **what a word means** — but they do not describe **where** each word appears.

## The Hidden Problem Inside Transformers

The Transformer receives a collection of embedding vectors — `E1`, `E2`, `E3`. These vectors contain meaning, but the model doesn't know:

- Which one came first?
- Which one came second?
- Which one came last?

To the Transformer, they look like an **unordered collection of vectors**.

This property is called **Permutation Invariance**.

  title="Permutation Invariance"

## Engineering Insight

When the authors of the **"Attention Is All You Need"** paper designed the Transformer, they intentionally removed recurrence (RNNs) to make computation highly parallel.

This made Transformers dramatically faster — but removing recurrence also removed the model's natural understanding of sequence.

So they had to answer a new question:

> **How can we tell the Transformer where each token appears?**

That question led to one of the most important ideas in modern AI: **Positional Information**.

## Key Takeaways

- Word order changes meaning.
- Embeddings represent meaning, not position.
- Transformers are naturally permutation invariant.
- Without positional information, the model cannot distinguish different word orders.
- Positional information is added to embeddings before the Transformer begins processing.
