## The Problem

Imagine two words — `Cat` and `Dog`.

Before training, their vectors might look like:

<TokenMapping
  title="Embeddings Before Training (Random)"
  fromLabel="Word"
  toLabel="Vector"
  items={[
    { from: "Cat", to: "[0.42, -0.81, 0.63, ...]"   },
    { from: "Dog", to: "[-1.12, 0.35, -0.27, ...]"  },
  ]}
/>

These numbers are random. They contain no understanding of animals.

So why, after training, do "Cat" and "Dog" become semantically similar?

---

## Intuition

Imagine two students. At the beginning of the school year, neither knows anything about programming.

As they attend the same classes, solve similar problems, and practice together, their knowledge gradually becomes similar.

Words behave in the same way. **Words appearing in similar contexts gradually learn similar representations.**

## Deep Explanation

Every time GPT makes a prediction, it computes a loss. Backpropagation then updates the model's parameters — and the Embedding Matrix is one of those learnable parameters.

<FlowDiagram title="How Embeddings Update" steps={[
  "Random Vector",
  "Prediction",
  "Loss",
  "Backpropagation",
  "Small Update to Embedding",
  "Repeat Millions of Times",
  "Meaningful Vector"
]} />

Each update is tiny. Meaning emerges from the accumulation of countless small improvements.

## Why Similar Words Become Close

Suppose GPT repeatedly sees sentences like:

- `The cat drinks milk.`
- `The dog drinks water.`
- `The kitten is sleeping.`
- `The puppy is sleeping.`

These words often appear in similar contexts. During training, their embedding vectors gradually become more alike.

The model isn't told that "Cat" and "Dog" are related — it **discovers** that relationship because similar contexts produce similar learning signals.

## Worked Example

Suppose GPT reads `The cat climbed the tree.`

Later, it reads `The dog climbed the hill.`

Both sentences have similar structures. The model updates the embeddings for **cat** and **dog** in similar ways.

<TokenMapping
  title="Embeddings for 'cat' Over Training"
  fromLabel="Stage"
  toLabel="Vector"
  items={[
    { from: "Initial",          to: "[0.42, -0.81, 0.63, ...]"  },
    { from: "After 1M steps",   to: "[-0.12, 0.47, 0.91, ...]"  },
    { from: "After 100M steps", to: "[-0.68, 1.02, 0.74, ...]"  },
  ]}
/>

After seeing millions of similar examples, the vectors for `cat` and `dog` become closer in semantic space. The model has learned a relationship — without anyone explicitly teaching it.

## Questions That Changed My Understanding

- Why don't engineers manually define word meanings?
- Why do similar words end up close together?
- How can tiny updates eventually create knowledge?
- Is meaning stored in a single number or across the entire vector?

---

## Key Takeaways

- Embeddings begin as random vectors.
- Training gradually updates those vectors.
- The Embedding Matrix learns along with the rest of the model.
- Words appearing in similar contexts develop similar embeddings.
- Meaning emerges through repeated optimization — not manual programming.
