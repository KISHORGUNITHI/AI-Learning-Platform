## Engineering Insight 1 — AI is Matrix Multiplication

Modern AI doesn't understand words. It understands **numbers**.

Almost every neural network layer performs one fundamental operation:

<FlowDiagram title="Core Neural Network Operation" steps={[
  "Input Vector",
  "Weight Matrix",
  "Matrix Multiplication",
  "Output Vector"
]} />

<MathBlock title="Linear Transformation">
  $$y = Wx + b$$
</MathBlock>

> Matrix multiplication is the computational engine of modern AI.

## Engineering Insight 3 — Batch & Epochs

Instead of training on the entire dataset at once, the dataset is divided into **batches**. After every batch has been processed, one **Epoch** completes.

<FlowDiagram title="Batch Training Flow" steps={[
  "Dataset",
  "Batch 1 → Forward Pass → Backpropagation → Update",
  "Batch 2 → Forward Pass → Backpropagation → Update",
  "Batch 3 → ...",
  "Epoch Complete",
  "Repeat for Next Epoch"
]} />

> Large datasets become manageable because training happens batch by batch.

---

## Engineering Insight 4 — Why Adam Optimizer?

Gradient Descent always moves downhill. Adam improves this by remembering previous updates and adapting future ones.

<Comparison
  title="Gradient Descent vs Adam"
  left={{
    label: "Plain Gradient Descent",
    points: [
      "Same learning rate for all parameters",
      "Simple updates",
      "Can be slow or unstable",
    ]
  }}
  right={{
    label: "Adam",
    points: [
      "Momentum from previous gradients",
      "Adaptive learning rate per parameter",
      "Faster and more stable training",
    ]
  }}
/>

> Most modern LLMs use **Adam** or **AdamW** for optimization.

## Engineering Insight 5 — Distributed Training

Large Language Models are too large for a single GPU. Engineers divide the work.

<Comparison
  title="Parallelism Strategies"
  left={{

> Multiple GPUs work together to train one massive model.

## Engineering Insight 7 — Random Embeddings Become Meaningful

When the model is created, embedding vectors are **random**. Training gradually adjusts them.

Eventually, words used in similar contexts develop similar vectors.

<FlowDiagram title="Embedding Evolution" steps={[
  "Random Vector",
  "Millions of Gradient Updates",
  "Meaningful Vector",
  "Similar Words Become Close in Space"
]} />

> Nobody manually assigns meaning to embeddings. Training discovers it automatically.

## Engineering Insight 8 — Embedding Lookup

The tokenizer produces a **Token ID**. That ID is simply an index into the **Embedding Matrix**. The corresponding vector is retrieved and sent into the Transformer.

<FlowDiagram title="Embedding Lookup" steps={[
  "Sentence",
  "Tokenizer",
  "Token ID (index)",
  "Embedding Matrix Row Lookup",
  "Embedding Vector",
  "Transformer"
]} />

> The embedding matrix is a giant learnable lookup table.

## Key Takeaways

- AI operates on numbers, not words.
- Matrix multiplication powers neural networks.
- GPUs accelerate parallel computation.
- Training happens batch by batch across epochs.
- Adam improves parameter updates with momentum and adaptive rates.
- Massive models require distributed training across many GPUs.
- Embeddings start random and become meaningful through training.
- Token IDs become vectors through embedding lookup.
