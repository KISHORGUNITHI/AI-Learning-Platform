> *"Backpropagation tells the model what to change. Gradient Descent actually performs the change."*

---

## The Problem

At the end of Backpropagation, every parameter now has a gradient.

The model knows:

- which parameters contributed to the mistake
- how much they contributed
- which direction they should move

But nothing has actually changed yet.

The parameters are still exactly the same.

Someone still needs to perform the update.

---

## Intuition

Imagine a teacher correcting an exam.

The teacher marks every mistake. The student now knows where the mistakes are and what needs improvement.

But unless the student actually studies and corrects those mistakes, nothing changes.

## Mathematics

Gradient Descent updates every parameter using:

<MathBlock title="Gradient Descent Update Rule">
  $$W_{\text{new}} = W_{\text{old}} - \eta \nabla L$$
</MathBlock>

where **W** is the parameter, **η** is the learning rate, and **∇L** is the gradient.

The equation simply means: move the parameter in the direction that reduces the loss.

---

## Learning Rate

The **Learning Rate** decides how large each update should be.

Think of walking downhill toward the lowest point.

The best learning rate balances speed and stability.

## Why Not Update Everything at Once?

Suppose the gradient suggests changing a parameter by a huge amount.

Would that always be a good idea? No.

A large update could destroy what the model has already learned.

Small, gradual improvements usually lead to better training. That's why optimization happens step by step.

---

## Adam Optimizer

In practice, modern LLMs rarely use plain Gradient Descent.

Instead, they use an improved optimizer called **Adam** (Adaptive Moment Estimation).

Adam keeps track of previous gradients and automatically adjusts the size of updates for each parameter.

<Comparison
  title="Gradient Descent vs Adam"
  left={{ label: "Plain Gradient Descent", points: ["Same learning rate for all parameters", "Can be slow to converge", "Sensitive to learning rate choice"] }}
  right={{ label: "Adam Optimizer", points: ["Adaptive learning rate per parameter", "Faster and more stable training", "Used by GPT, BERT, and most modern LLMs"] }}
/>

Almost every modern Transformer model — including GPT — is trained using Adam or its variant **AdamW**.

> Adam is an improved version of Gradient Descent that makes learning more efficient.

## Common Misconceptions

**Backpropagation updates the parameters.**

No. Backpropagation computes gradients. The optimizer updates the parameters.

**Bigger learning rates are always better.**

No. Very large learning rates often make training unstable.

**One optimization step trains the model.**

No. Training requires millions or billions of optimization steps.

**Adam changes the architecture.**

No. Adam only changes parameter values.

## Key Takeaways

- Backpropagation computes gradients.
- Gradient Descent updates the parameters.
- Learning Rate controls the size of each update.
- Training improves through millions of small optimization steps.
- Adam is the optimizer used by most modern LLMs.
