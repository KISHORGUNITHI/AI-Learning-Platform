## Step 1 — Collect Raw Text

Training begins with collecting enormous amounts of text: books, articles, research papers, Wikipedia, public websites, and programming code.

Together, this forms the training **corpus**.

---

## Step 2 — Clean the Data

The internet contains duplicate content, spam, broken text, and low-quality information.

Engineers clean the data before training.

> Better data leads to better learning.

---

## Step 3 — Tokenization

The sentence `The sky is blue.` is broken into tokens, then each token receives a unique Token ID.

The model understands numbers — not words.

---

## Step 4 — The Neural Network

The Token IDs enter GPT.

Initially, the network contains billions of **random parameters**. The architecture already exists, but no learning has happened yet.

---

## Step 5 — Forward Pass

The input travels through every layer.

The final layer produces one score for every vocabulary word.

## Step 6 — Logits

The output layer produces raw scores called **Logits**.

<TokenMapping

These scores indicate preference, not probability.

---

## Step 7 — Softmax

Softmax converts the logits into probabilities.

<TokenMapping
  title="After Softmax"
  fromLabel="Word"
  toLabel="Probability"
  items={[
    { from: "blue",     to: "0.84" },
    { from: "green",    to: "0.13" },
    { from: "dog",      to: "0.02" },
    { from: "computer", to: "0.01" },
  ]}
/>

Now the model can compare its prediction with the correct answer.

---

## Step 8 — Cross Entropy Loss

The model compares its prediction with the correct next token.

If the prediction is poor, the loss becomes large. If the prediction is good, the loss becomes small.

Loss answers one question:

> **"How wrong was my prediction?"**

---

## Step 9 — Backpropagation

The model traces the error backwards, computing gradients for every learnable parameter.

These gradients tell each parameter how much it contributed to the mistake.

---

## Step 10 — Gradient Descent / Adam

The optimizer receives the gradients and updates every parameter by a tiny amount.

<MathBlock title="Parameter Update">
  $$W_{\text{new}} = W_{\text{old}} - \eta \nabla L$$
</MathBlock>

Each update slightly reduces the loss. Learning occurs through millions and billions of tiny improvements.

---

## Step 11 — Repeat

One prediction teaches almost nothing. So the entire pipeline repeats — again, again, and again. Sometimes for **trillions of tokens**.

Eventually, the random parameters become learned parameters. That is what we call a trained GPT model.

## Common Misconceptions

**GPT memorizes every sentence.**

No. It learns statistical patterns through repeated optimization.

**One training step teaches the model.**

No. Training requires billions of iterations.

**The architecture changes during training.**

No. Only the parameter values change.

**GPT becomes intelligent instantly.**

No. Intelligence emerges gradually through continuous learning.

- GPT training is a continuous pipeline, not isolated steps.
