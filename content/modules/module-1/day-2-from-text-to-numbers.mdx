## The Problem

In the previous lesson, we collected and cleaned billions of pieces of text.

Now imagine giving the following sentence directly to a computer.

> **"Who are you?"**

Seems simple.

But here's the problem.

A computer doesn't understand English.

It doesn't know:

- what "Who" means
- what "are" means
- what "you" means

To the computer, these are simply symbols.

So before learning can begin, we must answer an important question:

> **How can a computer understand human language?**

---

## Intuition

Imagine talking to someone who only understands numbers.

You say:

> Hello

They stare at you.

Then you write:

```
101
```

Now they understand.

Nothing changed except the representation.

Computers work the same way.

They don't understand words. They understand numbers.

Every word must first be converted into numbers.

---

## Tokenization

This conversion process is called **Tokenization**.

Instead of reading words, the tokenizer breaks text into smaller pieces called **tokens**.

Example:

```
Who are you?
```

might become:

```
Who
↓
are
↓
you
↓
?
```

Each token is then assigned a unique numerical ID.

```
Who → 51
are → 884
you → 77
?   → 18
```

These numbers are not meaningful by themselves. They simply act as identifiers.

Think of them like student roll numbers.

A roll number identifies a student, but it doesn't describe their personality.

Similarly, a token ID identifies a token.

## Does GPT Understand These Numbers?

Not yet.

Many beginners think:

> "Once words become numbers, GPT understands them."

That's not true.

The numbers are only labels.

The model still doesn't know what they represent.

At this point, it is like giving someone a dictionary where every word has been replaced by an ID.

Without learning, those IDs have no meaning.

---

## The Neural Network

Now that the input has become numbers, those numbers enter the neural network.

But another surprise awaits us.

The neural network already exists **before training begins**.

Engineers design the architecture first. Then they initialize it with random values.

Think of it like constructing an empty school building.

The classrooms exist. The desks exist. The walls exist.

But no teaching has happened yet.

Similarly, GPT already has:

- Layers
- Connections
- Mathematical operations

But it contains no knowledge.

---

## Parameters

Inside the neural network, there are billions of adjustable values.

These are called **parameters**.

You can imagine them as tiny knobs.

During training, the model continuously adjusts these knobs to improve its predictions.

Knowledge is eventually stored in these parameters.

But today, every parameter is almost random.

---

## Worked Example

Imagine buying a brand-new guitar.

The guitar already has:

- Strings
- Frets
- Body

But nobody knows how to play it yet.

Training doesn't build the guitar.

Training teaches someone how to use it.

GPT works similarly.

Engineers build the architecture. Training teaches it patterns.

---

## Common Misconceptions

**Token IDs contain meaning.**

No. They are only identifiers. Meaning is learned later.

**Training creates the neural network.**

No. The architecture is designed before training begins. Training only changes the parameters.

**Parameters are knowledge from the beginning.**

No. Initially they are almost random numbers. Knowledge appears only after repeated training.

While learning this topic, ask yourself:

- Why can't computers understand words directly?
- Why do we need token IDs instead of raw text?
- If token IDs don't contain meaning, where does meaning come from?
- Why does the neural network already exist before training?

---

## Key Takeaways

- Computers understand numbers, not words.
- Tokenization converts text into tokens.
- Every token receives a unique numerical ID.
- Token IDs are identifiers, not meaning.
- The neural network exists before training.
- Training adjusts random parameters to gradually learn patterns.

---

## What's Next?

The model now has:

- Clean data 
- Token IDs 
- A neural network with random parameters 

So what happens next?

The model makes its **first prediction**.

Most of those predictions are completely wrong.

Surprisingly, that's exactly how learning begins.
